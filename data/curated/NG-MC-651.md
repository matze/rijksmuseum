## timeline
A warship four metres high and four and a half long, built in 1698, rigged, painted and
armed with 74 guns on three decks. It is a model at about one to twelve — which means the
ship it stands for would have been more than twelve times this size.

## closer
The stern. The arms of Zeeland, the year 1698, an encircled St George's cross, and four
‘RWR’ monograms along the taffrail.

## detail
It was never a toy or a shipyard tool. It stood in the council chamber of the Admiralty of
Zeeland in Middelburg, where its job was to impress: a demonstration of what the province
could build, carved with foliage, putti and an allegorical figure, and picked out in
yellow.

The work was divided the way a real ship's would be. Adriaen de Vriend and Adriaen
Davidsen made the model in Flushing, Cornelis Moerman did the carving, and Cornelis
Evertsen II oversaw the rigging. No actual vessel matches it exactly, although it may
loosely stand for the 94-gun Koning William.

## look
1. The lion with an English crown under the bowsprit.
2. The rigging, which is a working diagram of how such a ship was sailed.
3. The gunports, and how few of them are on the top deck.

## kids
This ship is a model. How much bigger was the real one? About twelve times.
