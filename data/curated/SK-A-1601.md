## timeline
A surgeon cuts a stone out of a patient's head, a folk cure for foolishness that the
picture treats as a joke on the fool who believes it.

## closer
The patient is tied to his chair. Around the round central scene, sketchy grotesque
figures crowd the edge.

## detail
The scene is based on what may be Bosch's own painting of the subject, now in Madrid,
inscribed "Meester snijt de keye ras, Mijne name is lubbert das". That reads "Master, cut
the stone out quickly. My name is Lubbert Das". The name Lubbert Das means Doltish Ninny,
which is the joke: the man asking to be cured of folly is called a fool.

The museum dates this panel to around 1550-1600 and attributes it to the manner of Bosch.
Onlookers sit at a table on the right, and a stone already cut from someone's head lies
on the table before them.

## look
1. The patient, tied into the chair while the surgeon works on his head.
2. The onlookers at the table, with a cut stone laid out.
3. The grotesque figures sketched around the edge.
