## timeline
Three kings arrive at the same hilltop from three directions and meet, with their horses,
their pages and a camel loaded with a wine barrel. It is carved in oak, seventy-six
centimetres high and twenty deep, and it is a traffic jam.

## closer
Count the horses' legs. Van Wesel has carved a crowd in twenty centimetres of depth and
got every one of them standing on the ground.

## detail
This is one section of a large carved altarpiece. The subject — the three Magi converging
on their way to Bethlehem — is a rare one, and it gave the carver what he plainly wanted: a reason to pack riders, animals and drapery into a
shallow box and still keep them all legible.

The oak keeps traces of its old polychromy but is essentially bare, which is not how it
was meant to be seen and is why the carving reads so clearly now. On the right a king has
just dismounted and his page is running to help him; on the left two shepherds watch the
procession go past with their sheep and goats on the slope below.
