## timeline
Marten Soolmans was twenty and had just married. He stands on a tiled floor in an
otherwise empty room, in black silk from hat to shoe, and the painting is over two
metres tall. Rembrandt made almost nothing else like it.

## closer
Nine-tenths of this picture is black. Count how many different blacks Rembrandt found in
it before you move on.

## detail
The portrait is one of a pair, painted in 1634 for the wedding of Soolmans and Oopjen
Coppit, and the two have hung side by side ever since — through Amsterdam and Paris
collections, unbroken, which is rare for a seventeenth-century pendant pair. Full length
and life size is rare for Rembrandt too; this was the format of court portraiture, and
these were the children of merchants.

They left French ownership in 2015, bought jointly by the Dutch State and the French
Republic for a hundred and sixty million euros. The two canvases belong half to the
Rijksmuseum and half to the Louvre, and travel together.
