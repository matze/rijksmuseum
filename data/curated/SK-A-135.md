## closer
Look at the collar from a metre away, then from arm's length. Close up it is a dozen
crossing slashes of grey and white; there is no drawing underneath any of it.

## detail
This is the high point of Hals's rough manner: loose strokes laid in varying thicknesses,
sometimes shaped with the wrong end of the brush, reading as speed close up and as
movement from across the room. Painters who work this way have no way back — nothing here
is corrected or glazed over.

Whether it is a portrait at all is disputed. The man shows his teeth, and polite company
of the 1620s did not laugh with its mouth open, which argues for a genre figure rather
than a sitter. Nobody has identified him. The museum bought the painting in 1816, and a
restoration in 1971 took off a discoloured varnish that had been flattening Hals's
colours.
