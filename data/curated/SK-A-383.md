## timeline
The painter of drunk families, sober: black suit, white collar, red curtain, a landscape
behind a balustrade. Steen put himself into dozens of his own pictures as the grinning
one at the back. Once, here, he painted himself as a respectable man.

## closer
Nothing in this picture says he is a painter. That is the choice, and it is the only time
he made it.

## detail
The format — curtain, parapet, distant view — is what Titian and Rembrandt had used, and
Steen is claiming it. He changed his mind repeatedly while doing so. Infrared shows three
versions of the collar: a very broad one, then a knotted cravat in the fashion of about
1668, then the plain white bef he settled on. The left eye was once larger and set
higher, and the mouth was once a wider grin, painted out in favour of the drier smile
that is there now.

The 1995 restoration is what made all this legible. Before it, an overpainted brown
background sat round his head like a cloud; underneath were the red curtain with its
tasselled cord and a landscape painted wet into wet that looks oddly like the work of his
father-in-law Jan van Goyen. The one loose end is the balustrade, which does not continue
on the left.
