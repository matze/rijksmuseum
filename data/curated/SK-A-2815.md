## timeline
Seven panels, painted in 1504, showing how a good Christian is supposed to help people
who need it: feeding the hungry, giving drink to the thirsty, clothing the naked, burying
the dead, sheltering the traveller, comforting the sick, ransoming the captive. The
setting is not the Holy Land but a Dutch town, with its own streets, doorways and
passers-by.

## closer
Christ is standing among the onlookers in every panel. Once you have found him in one,
you will find him in the rest.

## detail
The acts come from Matthew 25, where they decide the Last Judgement — which is why the
Judgement is painted in the upper centre of the middle panel. The series was probably
commissioned by the Holy Ghost confraternity of Alkmaar, whose members ran a house where
the sick were cared for and travellers put up: the seven scenes are the work they
actually did.

The panels have been attacked. Faces were cut away with a sharp tool, before 1582 and
probably during the iconoclasm of 1566; in 1582 two men painted the whole thing over in
black. When the museum, which bought the work in 1918, had it restored between 1971 and
1975, it decided not to fill the losses in. What you are looking at is the damage as well
as the painting.

## look
1. Christ, standing unremarked among the spectators in panel after panel.
2. The town itself: real doorways, real clothing, real Dutch weather.
3. The cut and missing faces, which have deliberately not been painted back.

## kids
