## timeline
The village of Zoutelande on Walcheren, seen from the top of a dune: sea on the left,
roofs and a mill on the right, and the whole thing built out of separate dabs of colour
that never touch. Painted between 1910 and 1912, a metre and a half wide.
region: 0.0400 0.4000 0.5000 0.5500

## closer
Walk towards it until the dots become a field, then take one step back. That distance is
what the painting is about.

## detail
This is pointillism, arrived in the Netherlands about twenty-five years after Paris
invented it. The method is to put unmixed colours side by side and let the eye do the
mixing, and it works best on exactly this sort of subject: strong light, flat land and a
great deal of green needing to stay alive across a metre and a half.

Hart Nibbrig signed it at the lower right. The dune the view is taken from is doing the
same work as the sky in a seventeenth-century Dutch landscape — it puts the horizon low
and lets the village sit in the middle distance, small.
