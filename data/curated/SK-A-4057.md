## timeline
She was long called Saskia van Uylenburgh, Rembrandt's wife, and the museum's own record
still says the sitter may be her. The title has changed because this is not strictly a
portrait: it is a tronie, a fantasy head, painted in 1633.

## closer
The clothes give it away. Nobody in Amsterdam dressed like this in the 1630s — the costume
is borrowed from earlier centuries.

## detail
The face has the domed forehead, slightly protruding eyes and pronounced chin that recur
in Rembrandt's goddesses and heroines of the same years. That is an argument for a type
rather than a likeness, and it is why the picture was renamed.

What is not in doubt is the jewellery. A thin veil over the hair, pearl earrings, a rope
of pearls at the throat, all painted at a size where you can see how each one is made of
two or three strokes.

## look
1. The veil, and how little paint it takes.
2. The pearls at the ear and the throat.
3. The costume, which belongs to no decade she lived in.
