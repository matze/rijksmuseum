## timeline
Not a child's toy. Petronella Oortman, married to the silk merchant Johannes Brandt, spent
the years between about 1686 and 1710 filling this cabinet — two and a half metres of it —
with rooms furnished to scale by the trades that furnished real ones. Wealthy Amsterdam
women kept houses like this the way their husbands kept cabinets of curiosities, and
showed them to visitors over a whole evening.

## closer
Look at the sides of the cabinet, in the leaf-work: her initials, B O.

## detail
Before you reach a single miniature the container is already a luxury object — an oak
carcass veneered in tortoiseshell and tin, on eight S-shaped scroll legs, with six
facetted panes in each front door. Her daughter Hendrina said her mother spent some 30,000
guilders on it, which at the time would have bought a canal house.

Jacob Appel painted the house in 1710, and that painting hangs in this same room; the
rooms have changed remarkably little since he recorded them. What is missing is the
people: the original dolls are gone. The state bought the house in 1821 and the
Rijksmuseum acquired it in 1875.

## look
1. The tortoiseshell and tin marquetry, and the initials B O on the sides.
2. The kitchen, and then each floor above it in turn.
3. Appel's painting of this house nearby, to see what has and has not survived.
