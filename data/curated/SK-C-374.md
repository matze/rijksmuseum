## timeline
The only militia piece Frans Hals ever painted outside Haarlem, and it nearly did not
get painted at all. He travelled to Amsterdam repeatedly; sitters failed to turn up; he
demanded they come to him instead; they refused and hired Pieter Codde to finish it.

## closer
Look along the row and find the seam. The left-hand men are alive in a way the

## detail
Hals was commissioned in 1633 on the strength of his Haarlem militia pieces, and the
picture is dated 1637 — four years for a job he had done in months before. Every sitter
paid for his own place on the canvas, which is one reason a group portrait of this kind
could stall: it was a dozen separate transactions pretending to be a painting.

The composition and roughly the seven figures on the left are held to be largely Hals's.
Codde matched the style closely enough that the join is a matter of argument rather than
of sight, but the brushwork on the left is looser and the faces there do more.
