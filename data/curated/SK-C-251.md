## timeline
A young woman stands in morning light, still in her blue night jacket, reading a letter
that arrived before she was dressed. Vermeer gives away nothing of what it says and
everything about how it is being read.

## closer
The blue is the whole picture. Once you notice how far it reaches — jacket, chair,
tablecloth, the shadows on the wall — the room stops being a room and becomes a colour.

## detail
This was the first Vermeer the Rijksmuseum had. It came with the Van der Hoop bequest
to the City of Amsterdam in 1854 and hung here from the museum's opening in 1885; it is
still the City's, on loan. The painted canvas is 46.5 by 39 centimetres — the sizes the
museum lists for it are the framed ones.

The map behind her is a real one — Holland and West Friesland, drawn by Balthasar
Florisz. van Berckenrode and published in 1620 — and Vermeer used it in other pictures
too. Because it hangs where the far corner of the room would be, the painting is the
only interior of his in which no corner, no floor and no ceiling can be seen at all: the
woman stands against a flat wall of paper and plaster.
region: "The map behind her is a real one" 0.3000 0.0400 0.6300 0.3700

Whether she is pregnant has been argued over for a century. The loose jacket was the
fashion, and Dutch painting of the period almost never showed pregnancy, so the
roundness may be the cut of the cloth. The picture declines to settle it, which is
consistent with everything else it declines to settle.
