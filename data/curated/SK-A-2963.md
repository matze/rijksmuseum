## timeline
A judge of the Madrid court, painted by Goya in 1823, standing with his hands in his
trouser pockets and his shirt open at the neck. He is not posing. This is the only Goya in
a public collection in the Netherlands.

## closer
The hands in the pockets. In 1823 that is not how a judge had himself painted.

## detail
There is another man underneath. Researchers using X-ray fluorescence found a second
portrait beneath this one: a senior French officer in uniform, medals across his chest,
painted around 1810 during the Peninsular War, possibly a general and possibly Joseph
Bonaparte, whom Napoleon had made king of Spain.

Goya lived through both regimes and survived both. After 1820 a French general on your
easel was dangerous property, and the surface we can see dates from 1823. The painting was
bought by the Vereniging Rembrandt in 1922 and given to the museum.
