## timeline
An ordinary Delft house front, painted with the attention normally reserved for
altarpieces. Two women work, two children play on the pavement, and nothing else happens.
For its time a portrait of ordinary houses was an unusual thing to want to make, and it is
unusual within Vermeer's own work too.

## closer
The old walls are almost tangible — brick, whitewash, cracks, and repairs. Vermeer painted
the repairs.

## detail
The museum places the view at Vlamingstraat 40–42 in Delft. Vermeer's aunt, Ariaentgen
Claes, lived in the house on the right with her children from around 1645 until her death
in 1670, which gives the picture a family address rather than a picturesque one.

The composition is held together entirely by masonry and shutters: two doorways, two
windows, a passage between the houses, and a strip of sky. Everything that would normally
carry a painting — event, gesture, expression — has been removed, and the balance still
holds.
