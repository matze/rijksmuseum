## timeline
A crowded street scene, nearly two and a half metres wide, pits Carnival against Lent:
feasting against fasting, excess against moderation.

## closer
The wood tells part of the story. Three oak planks make the support, and tree-ring dating
says the youngest ring grew in 1569, so the panel was probably painted no earlier than
about 1594.

## detail
A little man playing the bagpipes is carried out of the kitchen on a table, and his
pitcher of drink has fallen over. Around the fireplace the work is all Shrove Tuesday,
and the cleaning of fish points to Lent. A woman carries a round table on her head with
just two fish on it, the meagre Lenten diet.

In the foreground two couples, monks and nuns, dance the dance of fools. An inscription
reads "Dit is den dans van Luther met zijn nonne": this is the dance of Luther with his
nun. The museum's own curators doubt the work is even after Bosch. Only the musicians
inside the giant bagpipe recall him, and they suggest the composition was invented in
Antwerp around 1560.

## look
1. The bagpiper carried out of the kitchen on the left.
2. The dancing monks and nuns in the foreground.
3. The giant bagpipe on the right, with a lute-player inside.

## kids
Count how many people are eating and how many are dancing. Which side is winning?
