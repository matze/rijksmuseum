## timeline
A saint painted as a wealthy woman. She sits under a tree with her jar of ointment in her
lap, in a landscape of mountains and jagged rock that exists nowhere in the Netherlands.
Everything else about her — the sleeves, the jewellery, the composure — belongs to Haarlem
around 1530, and to money.

## closer
Her bodice is embroidered with Hebrew lettering, and her shawl carries a pattern of
Haarlem damask — the local cloth trade, worked into a biblical scene.

## detail
The ointment jar is how you know who she is. Without it this would read as the portrait of
a rich woman sitting out of doors, which is very close to what Van Scorel has painted.
region: "The ointment jar is how you know who she is" 0.3900 0.5500 0.1250 0.2700

The panel has been confiscated twice. It hung in the Commandery of St John in Haarlem
until 1572, when it was taken for the city after the iconoclastic revolt, and it was taken
again in 1804, this time by the Dutch state, catalogued then as the work of an unknown
hand. It has been shown as a highlight of the national collection ever since.
