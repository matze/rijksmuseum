## timeline
Signed and dated 1626, from Rembrandt's Leiden years, and the size of a sheet of paper.
Tobit has gone blind; his wife Anna keeps them alive by sewing for the neighbours. She has
come home with a goat she was paid with, he hears it bleating and accuses her of stealing
it, and he is praying to be allowed to die.

## closer
His hands. The whole accusation and the whole apology are in a pair of folded hands and a
face that cannot see the animal it is arguing about.

## detail
Rembrandt fills the room with the evidence of their poverty: a string of onions and a
birdcage by the window, shelves of household things along the back wall, a basket in a
niche, a spool of yarn left on a chair, a stick on the floor beside him, a small dog at
the fire. Anna stands holding the goat and says nothing.

He was barely twenty when he painted it, and the picture is already about the wrong
conclusion drawn in a moment. In the Book of Tobit the couple's fortunes are restored, but
that is not the part he chose.

## look
1. Tobit's folded hands, and the direction his blind face is turned.
2. The onions and the birdcage at the window.
3. Anna's expression as she is accused.

## kids
He thinks she stole the goat. She did not. How can you tell she is upset?
