## timeline
A few expensive things on a stone ledge, in a niche, in almost no light: an ornamented
silver jug with a lid, a römer of white wine on a gilt stand, a Wan-Li porcelain bowl with
lemons and oranges, and a watch. Kalf gives them the darkness a portrait painter would
give a face.

## closer
The peeled lemon in the bowl, and the whole one lying in front of it. They are the
brightest things in the picture, and they are there to make the silver read as silver.

## detail
Every object is imported or expensive: Chinese export porcelain, Amsterdam silverwork,
southern fruit, a timepiece. This is a portrait of what a Dutch merchant household could
afford in the 1650s, painted with the same care other painters gave to faces.

The watch is the only object that is not simply wealth. In still-life painting of the
period a clock is the standard reminder that the meal, the fruit and the owner are all on
a clock. The museum bought the canvas at an Amsterdam sale on 29 October 1821.

## look
1. The curl of lemon peel in the porcelain bowl.
2. The figures worked into the silver of the jug, and the gilt stand under the glass.
3. The watch on its ribbon, half in shadow at the front edge of the ledge.
