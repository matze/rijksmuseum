## timeline
A painter looks out of an oval gold frame that is itself painted, past a curtain that is
also painted, over a ledge where his palette and brushes are lying. Troost had been an
actor before he was a painter, and this is a piece of stagecraft.

## detail
Troost worked as an actor and a scene painter before making his living with a brush, and
what he understood from that trade was illusion. The oval, the drape, the pedestal and
the objects laid on it are there to build depth in front of a head that is otherwise
simply a head, seen — as he tells you — in a mirror.

He signed and dated it 1739: C. Troost ipse fecit, made by himself. The showpiece is the
jabot at his throat, the frill of fine batiste on his shirt, which the museum's own note
points out is a single stroke of white paint.
