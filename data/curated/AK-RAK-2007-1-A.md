## timeline
Two and a half metres of carved wood, snarling, mid-stride, with most of the paint gone.
He is Misshaku kongō, called Agyō, and he and his twin stood either side of the gate of
the Iwayaji temple in Shimane, in western Japan.
region: 0.3700 0.0700 0.3600 0.2200

## closer
Everything about him is aimed at somebody walking through a gate. Stand where that person
would have stood.

## detail
Guardians of this kind come in pairs and are placed at an entrance, one to each side; the
museum holds both of these. The carving is dated to the fourteenth century or thereabouts
and is wood with what survives of its polychromy — which here means bare grain, open
splits, and patches of white and red where the paint held.
region: "patches of white and red where the paint held" 0.2500 0.3700 0.4500 0.3000

He is 239.5 centimetres tall and weighs 175 kilos with his base. Nothing about the pose
is at rest: the weight is on the forward foot, one arm is up, the other is thrown out
low, and the drapery is carved as though it were still moving.
