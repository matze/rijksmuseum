## timeline
A preacher of seventy-six, standing life-size, gloves in one hand and the other on his
chest, looking straight at you. Johannes Wtenbogaert led the Remonstrants, the losing side
of the religious conflict that split the young Dutch Republic. Rembrandt painted him in
1633 and wrote his age on the canvas: 'Aet:.76'.

## closer
The gaze. Everything else in the picture — the black, the fur, the book — is arranged so
that you meet it.

## detail
It was not the church that ordered this. Abraham Anthonisz Recht, an Amsterdam merchant
and an admirer, commissioned the portrait and hung it in his own house — a private
statement of loyalty to a man on the wrong side of an argument the state had settled.

The open book leaning against the wall marks him as a man of the Word. Nobody has
identified the text. The museum bought the painting in December 1992 for £4,180,000, with
the Vereniging Rembrandt, three funds, the Dutch state and private collectors.

## look
1. The face, and the age written beside it.
2. The gloves held in the right hand — a standing convention for a man of standing.
3. The open book and the broad-brimmed hat set down in front of it.
