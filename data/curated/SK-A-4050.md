## timeline
Rembrandt at fifty-five, in a white turban, holding a bundle of letters and wearing a
sword under his cloak. The turban and the sword and the manuscript belong to the Apostle
Paul. The face is entirely his own, and he has not flattered it.

## closer
He is not playing the saint so much as lending him a face — and the face he had to lend
was this one.

## detail
Paul is identified in art by a sword and by the epistles he wrote, and both are here:
the blade shows where the cloak falls open, the pages are in his hands. Putting his own
head on the apostle was a way of pulling the viewer up close to a figure who would
otherwise stay at scripture's distance.

It is one of more than forty painted self-portraits, and among the last. Ernst van de
Wetering read the surface as wet-on-wet work: the turban gone at speed, the lit forehead
built up thick enough for the paint itself to make the wrinkles. The signature and the
date 1661 are written across the shadowed wall on the left.
