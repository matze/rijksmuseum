## timeline
In 1888 Paris had become too busy for Van Gogh and in the spring he left for Arles, in
the south of France, where the colours of the landscape took hold of him. Here the
horizon is set high, so the yellow of the wheat takes almost the whole picture, and the
blue of the hills and the sky is worked up to stand against it.

## closer
Stand back for the yellow, then come close: almost none of the field is painted yellow.
It is orange, red, green and blue, laid on stroke by stroke.

## detail
He was about thirty-five when he moved south, and the stay was prolific: in under 444 days
at Arles he made about a hundred drawings, more than two hundred paintings, and wrote
more than two hundred letters. The wheat harvest of June 1888 gave him a run of canvases,
and on 21 June he described them to his brother Theo as landscapes "yellow — old gold —
done quickly, quickly, quickly, and in a hurry just like the harvester who is silent
under the blazing sun, intent only on the reaping".

The sky is laid in long horizontal strokes and the field in short upright ones, so the
two halves of the picture are different weather to the hand as well as to the eye. What
stands between them is a thin strip of the world: a white farmhouse with an orange roof,
a haystack, sheds, a row of dark trees at the right-hand edge, and the blue line of the
hills behind all of it.
