## timeline
A man in a red coat points out of a window at a fort. The fort is Elmina, the
headquarters of the Dutch slave trade in West Africa, and he ran it for twelve years. The
second figure in the painting was enslaved, and the museum's title now says so.
region: 0.2100 0.1000 0.3600 0.8000

## closer
Two men, one painting, two entirely different reasons for being in it. The gesture out of
the window connects them.

## detail
Jan Pranger was director-general of the Dutch Gold Coast from 1730 to 1742. On the voyage
home he travelled by way of Suriname with, in the records of the time, "170 Slaves, coming
from the coast of Guinea" aboard. They were left in Paramaribo. One person was not: he
came on to Amsterdam as Pranger's servant, and it was in Amsterdam that Frans van der Mijn
painted the two of them together in 1742.
region: "One person was not: he came on to Amsterdam as Pranger's servant" 0.0900 0.2800 0.2100 0.6200

The picture is over two metres tall and is built entirely around Pranger: the light, the
red coat, the pointing hand, the documents and inkstand on the table. The other man
stands behind him in shadow, holding a parasol, and is not named anywhere.
