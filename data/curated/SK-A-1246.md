## timeline
A man of forty leaning over the back of his chair with a stick in his hand, painted about
1637, the year he married. Jan van de Poll went on to be burgomaster of Amsterdam seven
times. Hals painted him and his wife in Haarlem, and these are the only Amsterdam
marriage pendants he ever made.

## detail
Van de Poll and Duifje van Gerwen married in 1637 and travelled to Haarlem to sit — the
suggestion probably came from her uncle, who had appeared as a captain in one of Hals's
Haarlem militia pieces. Hals was in Amsterdam at the time trying to finish the Meagre
Company, whose sitters had refused to make the same journey; this couple went instead,
and got the portraits.

For well over a century the pair carried the wrong names. The museum's first director
attached them in 1885 to a different Amsterdam couple; the wills of that couple's heirs show
the pictures cannot have passed down that line, and comparison with two later portraits
of Van de Poll — as a militia colonel, painted by other hands — settled who this is.
