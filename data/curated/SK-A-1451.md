## timeline
Two and a half metres of food: game, poultry, artichokes, cabbages, meat, jugs and
plates, with two kitchen maids working among it. The subject of the painting is none of
that. It is the small scene through the arch, where Christ is visiting Mary and Martha.

## closer
The picture is built as a trap. Look at the food for as long as you like, then find the
part it is keeping you from.

## detail
Bueckelaer signed and dated it 1566. The construction — an overwhelming worldly
foreground with a small religious scene set far back — was the speciality that made his
name, and the meaning depends on the imbalance: the more the kitchen holds you, the
better the argument works. He was an Antwerp painter, and his market and kitchen pieces
fed into the whole later development of still life in northern Europe.

In the story, Martha is busy with the housework while her sister Mary sits and listens to
Christ. That is what is happening through the arch, in the middle of the picture, at a
tenth of the scale of the plucked chickens.
