## timeline
A young man at the right is dancing an egg into a chalk circle without breaking it, and
then covering it with a wooden bowl. That was the game. The room is a brothel, and the
whole painting is a warning dressed up as a party.

## detail
The egg dance was played for laughs at fairs and in taverns: roll an egg inside a drawn
circle with your feet, keep it whole, then flip a bowl over it. Aertsen paints the game
being attempted and everyone else occupied with something less innocent — the man at the
left raising a jug over his head, the couple sprawled across the bench beside him, the
bagpiper at the fire.

Pictures like this were sold as entertainment and defended as morality. The date 1552 is
painted on the chimney breast above the fire. The panel is nearly a metre and three
quarters wide: a tavern interior given the scale of a history painting.
