## timeline
A writing desk from about 1775 by Jean-Henri Riesener, the cabinetmaker who supplied the
French court. Oak underneath; on top of that, pictures made entirely out of other woods,
gilt bronze at every edge, and a marble slab behind a pierced brass rail.
region: 0.3550 0.1650 0.3050 0.1500

## closer
Nothing here is painted. Every line, petal and shadow is a differently coloured piece of
wood cut and laid in.

## detail
The technique is marquetry: veneers of maple and other woods glued over an oak carcase in
a jigsaw fine enough to draw with. The middle panel of the raised back, behind two tambour
doors, shows a lyre, books and an inkwell; the drawer front and the shelf below carry
sprays of roses; the side fields are filled with lattice and rosettes.

The front legs are set on the diagonal, which is what keeps a piece this loaded with
ornament from looking like a box. Riesener stamped his name under the marble at the right
rear corner — furniture of this rank was signed like a painting.
