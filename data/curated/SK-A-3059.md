## timeline
A mother holds a limp child across her lap. The child is awake and looking out of the
picture; nobody else is. Metsu painted it in Amsterdam within a year or two of the plague
of 1663, which killed one person in ten.
region: 0.5850 0.3550 0.1900 0.1750

## closer
The child's eyes are the only ones open in the room, and they are looking at you rather
than at the mother.

## detail
The pose is a pietà — the Virgin with the dead Christ across her knees — moved into a
Dutch room and given a living child. Metsu makes the reference explicit by hanging a
small painting of the Crucifixion on the wall above them, in a dark arched frame.
region: "a small painting of the Crucifixion on the wall above them" 0.5950 0.0200 0.3200 0.2450

Everything else is ordinary and quiet: an earthenware pot with a spoon in it on the
table, clothes and a cap thrown over the chair, a rolled wall map behind. The map carries
the signature. The canvas is 32 by 27 centimetres, which is smaller than most people
expect from a reproduction.
