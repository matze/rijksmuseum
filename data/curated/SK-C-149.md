## timeline
A mother goes through her child's hair for lice, by the window, in a plain room with a
box bed and a tiled floor. De Hooch gives the job the same patient attention the mother
is giving it, and then opens a door onto a sunlit room beyond.

## closer
The whole painting is built to make you look through that doorway, and then come back to
what is actually happening in the near room.

## detail
De Hooch made a speciality of the doorkijkje, the through-view: a second room, seen past
a door frame, lit from a source the near room cannot reach. Here it does more than show
off. The far room is bright, empty and quiet; the near one is shadowed and busy with an
unglamorous chore, and the two rooms are held together by a line of sunlight lying across
both floors.
region: "The far room is bright, empty and quiet" 0.1350 0.2300 0.2000 0.5600

The furnishings are ordinary and exactly observed: Delft blue tiles along the wall, a
box bed with its pillows stacked, a wicker basket, and in the right foreground a
kakstoel — a child's potty chair. A dog sits watching the open door.
