## timeline
A shaft of sunlight comes through the cloud and lands on a barn and a clump of trees,
which makes the storm behind it twice as bad. Under the bridge, boats; on the road, a
cart and a few people walking; in the distance to the right, a church tower.

## closer
Find the edge of the light. Rembrandt lets it fall on one small part of the picture and
leaves the rest in half-tone.

## detail
Rembrandt painted very few landscapes, and most of them are imaginary mountain country.
This one is built out of thoroughly Dutch parts — a stone bridge, a hay barn, a waterway,
a tower — and is very probably still invented, assembled for the light rather than
observed from a spot.

It is a small panel, under thirty centimetres high, and it has been treated as a major
work for two centuries: a French sale catalogue of 1817 already called its rarity and
perfection remarkable despite its small size.

## look
1. The lit barn and trees at the centre, and how little else is lit.
2. The boats on the water under the bridge.
3. The church tower on the horizon at the right.
