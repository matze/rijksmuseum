## timeline
Father is singing with a full glass in his hand, mother and grandmother are singing with
him, and the children are learning the tune. A sheet of paper pinned above the fireplace
gives the moral: as the old sing, so the young pipe.

## closer
It looks like an argument for a good evening. Read the note on the mantelpiece and it
turns into a warning.

## detail
Steen painted this in 1668, and the proverb it illustrates was old already. Everything
in the room is an example being set: the man playing bagpipes, the old man's raised
roemer of wine, the boy leaning out of the window on the left, the two smallest children
at the front being handed a pipe to try.
region: "the two smallest children at the front being handed a pipe to try" 0.5650 0.4900 0.2200 0.3700

The trouble with the argument is that the evening looks like a good one. Steen has made
the wrong example far more attractive than the lesson, which is why the picture has
outlasted the proverb it was painted to carry.
