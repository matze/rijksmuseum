## timeline
Oopjen Coppit sat for this a year after her wedding, aged twenty-three and expecting her
first child. She came from an old and wealthy Amsterdam family; her husband's was new
money. The two portraits are the alliance, hung on a wall.

## detail
The commission was for two full-length life-size canvases from the leading portraitist
in the city, which is how a merchant family announced itself in 1634. She is the eldest
of three daughters; he is twenty, and dead within seven years.

Everything expensive here is black. What reads as one dark mass is silk, velvet and a
sheer black veil laid over each other, and Rembrandt separates them by nothing but the
way each takes the light. The white is confined to the collar and the cuffs, and the
gold chain running out of her hand is close to the only warm colour in the canvas.
