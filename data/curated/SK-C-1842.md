## timeline
The other half of the smallest pair Rembrandt ever painted: a woman in black with a
pleated ruff, turned to the left, twenty centimetres of panel. He inscribed her age and
his name and the year on it — 'Æ 70 / Rembrandt / f. 1635'.

## closer
Seventy years old, and he tells you so on the picture. Then look at how much of her face
he actually paints.

## detail
Jaapgen Caerlsdr was the wife of Jan Willemsz van der Pluym, whose portrait hangs beside
hers. The two were made as a pendant pair and have stayed together, which for
seventeenth-century marriage portraits is closer to luck than to planning.

Painted the same year as the Man in Oriental Dress in this room, and by any measure of
ambition the opposite of it: no costume, no theatre, no light effects. Just a face the
painter knew.

## look
1. The inscription giving her age as seventy.
2. The ruff, and how it is built out of grey rather than white.
3. Her husband's portrait beside her, the same size and the same year.
