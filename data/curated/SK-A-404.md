## timeline
A woman in white satin stands with her back to us. A seated officer is saying something
to her, one hand raised; a second woman drinks from a glass and does not look up. What is
going on here has never been settled.

## closer
Her face is turned away and the picture is built so that it stays that way. Everything
you want to know is behind that head.

## detail
The old title calls it a father admonishing his daughter, which the picture does not
support: the man is an officer, still holding his hat as though he has just come in, and
the woman with the glass is nobody's chaperone. The museum's own note goes no further
than saying the reason for the gathering remains obscure. Ter Borch liked scenes whose
point cannot be pinned down, and this is the most unpinnable of them.

What he could do without ambiguity is cloth. The satin dress is the reason people stop
here — the folds are painted in long dragged strokes of grey, white and pale gold that
change direction with the fall of the skirt, and nothing else in the room is given
anything like the same attention.
