## timeline
Rembrandt was twenty-four and still in Leiden. The prophet sits on a rocky slope with his
head propped on his left hand while, down at the left, Jerusalem burns and soldiers move
through it. Beside him on the stone lie a gold dish and jug and some books: what he
managed to carry out of the temple.

## closer
The panel is small, 58 by 46 centimetres. Nearly all of it is dark, and the light is spent
on an old man's forehead and the gold at his elbow.

## detail
The subject is not the catastrophe but the reaction to it. The city is painted small and
far off; the figure is close, still, and doing nothing at all. Rembrandt would spend the
rest of his life on that decision — the moment after the event rather than the event.

It is one of the very few paintings from the Leiden years that is regularly called a
masterpiece, and it hangs here in the room devoted to the young Rembrandt, before
Amsterdam, before the commissions, before the Night Watch.
