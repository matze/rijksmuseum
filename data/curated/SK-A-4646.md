## timeline
A pie with a whole turkey reassembled on top of it — skin, wings and head — set among
oysters, lemons, olives, nuts, a nautilus cup and a Chinese porcelain bowl of grapes.
Nobody ate like this. The table is an argument about what its owner could afford.

## detail
Claesz signed and dated it 1627, monogram and year cut into the blade of the knife at the
front of the table. It is among the largest and most ambitious things he painted, and it
belongs to the moment when Dutch still life turned from modest breakfast pieces to
displays of imported luxury.

Much of what is on the table came a long way. The fruit bowl is Chinese Wan-li
porcelain; the cup beside it is a tropical shell set in a gilt mount; the flagons are
pewter and silver. The white damask, the oriental carpet under it and the low viewpoint
are all doing the same work as the imports.
