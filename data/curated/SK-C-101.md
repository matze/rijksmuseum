## timeline
Amsterdam's town hall on the Dam, painted in 1693 in clean morning light, with the square
in front of it full of people going about their business. The building is now the Royal
Palace, and it was the loudest statement the city ever made about itself.

## closer
The building is drawn as if by an architect and the square as if by a reporter. Two
different kinds of attention in one painting.

## detail
The town hall went up in the middle of the seventeenth century, when Amsterdam had more
money than any city in Europe, and it was designed to say so: classical, symmetrical,
enormous, with a cupola over the middle. Berckheyde painted it repeatedly, and here he
takes it head on and gives it two-thirds of the canvas.

Everything else is compressed around it. The Nieuwe Kerk crowds in at the right, the
gabled houses of the Damrak at the left, and across the paving are pedlars, a dog, a
wheelbarrow, riders and a knot of people bargaining. The signature and the date are at
the lower left.
