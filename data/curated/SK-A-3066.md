## timeline
An old woman sits with a large book open on her lap, reading. She is probably Rembrandt's
mother, Neeltgen Willemsdr van Zuydtbroeck, and she is probably standing in for the
prophetess Anna. Both of those are 'probably', and the picture is better for it.

## closer
The hand on the page. It is the brightest thing in the painting, brighter than her face.

## detail
Rembrandt signed it 'RHL 1631' — the monogram of his Leiden years, before he became simply
Rembrandt. His mother sat for him repeatedly in that period, which is how a scriptural
figure comes to have the face of somebody from Leiden.

The text is unreadable, deliberately: what the picture is about is an old woman deep in a
book, not a passage of scripture. He painted the fur, the velvet and the gold of the
binding with far more attention than the words.

## look
1. The lit hand resting on the open page.
2. The monogram 'RHL 1631' at the lower left.
3. Her face, which is turned away from the light he gave the book.
