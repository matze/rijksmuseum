## timeline
Water falls out of a mill race, a wheel turns, a woman stands in her doorway and a man
rolls a barrel across the yard. Hobbema had drawn this mill in the eastern Netherlands
and brought the drawing home to paint from.

## closer

## detail
Hobbema learned landscape from Jacob van Ruisdael, whose work this resembles in almost
every particular except mood. Ruisdael's woods are older and darker; Hobbema's saplings
run up into the light and the leaves at the top of them are lit from behind. The panel
is small, 60.5 by 85 centimetres, and most of it is trees.

The mill is the same building he used in several pictures, worked up from a drawing made
on the spot. What changes between versions is everything around it — the weather, the
water, where the light falls — which is a landscape painter's way of saying that the
subject was never the mill.
