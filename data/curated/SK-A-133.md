## timeline
Isaac Massa and Beatrix van der Laen married in Haarlem on 25 April 1622, and Hals painted
them sitting on the ground under a tree, leaning on each other, grinning at us. Married
couples of the period were painted upright, formal, and usually on two separate canvases.
This is what the alternative looked like.

## closer
Her hand on his shoulder. In a marriage portrait of 1622 that gesture is the whole
argument of the picture.

## detail
The museum's description records a thistle at the left of the couple and ivy at the right,
and behind them, at the right, elegant couples strolling in a garden with a fountain
beside a country house. That garden is doing the work a formal portrait would have given
to columns and drapery: it places the sitters in a world of leisure and property.

The identification is likely rather than certain — the museum titles it 'Portrait of a
Married Couple, Likely Isaac Abrahamsz Massa and Beatrix van der Laen'. It was once
supposed to show Hals and his own wife, which it does not.

## look
1. Her right hand, resting on his shoulder.
2. The thistle at the left and the ivy at the right.
3. The garden party in the distance, and the fountain beyond it.
