## timeline
A swan defends her nest against a dog, wings out, hissing, at close to life size — the
canvas is 144 by 171 centimetres. Asselijn gave an animal picture the scale and front-on
force of a history painting, and it works on anyone who walks past it without reading a
word.

## closer
The feathers nearest you are laid on thickly enough to cast their own shadow. The dog, by
contrast, is barely there.

## detail
Someone later decided the swan was Johan de Witt, the Grand Pensionary, defending Holland
against its enemies, and wrote the reading onto the painting: ‘de Raad-Pensionaris’ under
the swan, ‘Holland’ on the egg, ‘de viand van de staat’ above the dog. Asselijn did not
put them there — he died in 1652, and the inscriptions are now thought to date from the
1750s, from a pamphlet war. His own mark is the monogram ‘JA’ at the lower right.

It was under that political meaning that the picture became the first purchase of the
Nationale Konst-Gallery, the forerunner of this museum, in 1800, for a hundred guilders.
The collection you are standing in began with a hissing bird.

## look
1. The three inscriptions, which turn a swan into a statesman.
2. The near feathers, thick enough to throw shadows onto the ones behind.
3. The dog, deliberately vague — he is a threat, not a subject.

## kids
The swan is much bigger than the dog. Who do you think wins?
