## timeline
A frozen waterway with a whole town on it: skaters, sledges, a horse, tents pitched on
the ice, ships stuck fast until spring. Avercamp painted the same weather all his life
and by about 1620 he was painting it like this — the land almost gone, the people
enormous by comparison.

## closer

## detail
Avercamp's earlier ice scenes are bird's-eye views crowded with tiny figures — his
Winter Landscape with Ice Skaters, elsewhere in this museum, is one. By 1620 that had
opened out into broad frozen channels and flooded fields, with the trees and buildings
pushed to the margins and the figures given the picture.

The people in the immediate foreground are the largest and the most sharply drawn, and
they are a cross-section: different classes on the same ice, each doing what its class
did there. Further back the drawing softens deliberately, and the groups are strung out
along diagonals that pull the eye into the distance.
