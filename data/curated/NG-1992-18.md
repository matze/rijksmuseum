## timeline
A Javanese man sits cross-legged, naked, holding a diesel engine in his lap and looking
down at it. The sculpture stood from 1914 at the entrance of an Amsterdam firm that
exported machines to the Dutch East Indies. It is 443 kilos of limestone and it is an
advertisement.

## closer
Look at where his eyes are. Then look at what the sculpture makes him wear, and what it

## detail
The company was Lindeteves-Stokvis, and the building was on the J.W. Brouwersplein. The
figure is the colony and the engine is the trade; the pairing is the whole message, and
the message was that the Netherlands was bringing progress to Indonesia.

Everything in the carving argues that. The man is nude and seated on the ground, which is
how nineteenth-century Europe drew people it thought of as natural rather than modern.
The engine is precisely detailed, hard-edged, and the one thing in the work that is given
a manufacturer's finish. He is looking at it with admiration.
