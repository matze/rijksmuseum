## timeline
Helena van der Schalcke was two years old when Ter Borch painted her, standing full length
in white silk with a wicker basket over her arm and a carnation in her hand. The panel is
about the size of a sheet of paper.

## closer
The white dress is not white. Come close enough to see the pink and the blue-grey he used
to keep it alive.

## detail
She is dressed as a small adult, which is what seventeenth-century children were dressed
as; her parents, the cloth merchant Gerard van der Schalcke and Johanna Bardoel, were
painted by Ter Borch too. The carnation in her hand is a standard motif in portraits of
the period, read as resurrection and the hope of eternal life.

That reading is worth holding onto. Helena died in 1671, at twenty-five, and the picture
of her at two is the one that survived.
