## timeline
Charley Toorop painted herself in 1933 at Bergen in North Holland: a hard three-quarter
turn, bobbed hair, red lipstick, and a light that gives her no help at all. She is looking
straight out, and she has not made herself likeable.

## closer
The window behind her is dark, and an electric lamp is reflected in it. That lamp is the
light falling on her face.

## detail
Three roses, each a different colour, are painted at the lower left. They are loose and
alive next to a face built out of flat, deliberate planes, and the contrast is the
picture's whole argument about what a portrait is for.

The frame is flat and undecorated, and she painted the inscription 'Bergen Zelfportret'
onto the work. The museum bought it in 2021 with support from a private company — a recent
addition to a collection whose Dutch holdings mostly stop three centuries earlier.

## look
1. The lamp reflected in the black window.
2. The three roses, painted in a different manner from the face.
3. The edge of the collar, which is the only soft thing she allows herself.
