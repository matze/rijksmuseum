## timeline
A girl in a kimono, lying back on a bench: probably Geesje Kwak, who was sixteen. Breitner
photographed and painted her repeatedly in red and white kimonos, out of the enthusiasm
for Japanese prints that ran through European painting of the 1890s.

## closer
The white is barely white — pink, grey and pale blue, laid on wet into wet.

## detail
The canvas is smaller than it photographs, 59 by 57 centimetres, and almost all of it is
spent on the fall of the cloth in grey studio light. The background is left barely
resolved.

Breitner sketched this composition in a letter to his fellow painter Willem Witsen, which
survives in the Witsen archive in The Hague. The painting reached the museum through the
Drucker-Fraser bequest, part of a group of more than seventy paintings.

## look
1. The white cloth: pink, grey and pale blue worked wet into wet.
2. Her expression, which Breitner kept exactly as it came.
3. How little attention the background gets by comparison.
