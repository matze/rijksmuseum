## timeline
Five and a half metres of celebration dinner. On 18 June 1648 the Amsterdam crossbowmen
sat down in their own hall to mark the Peace of Münster, which ended eighty years of war
with Spain, and had themselves painted doing it. Captain Cornelis Jansz Witsen holds the
guild's silver drinking horn; Lieutenant Johan Oetgens van Waveren is shaking his hand.

## closer
The handshake at the centre is the subject. Everything else in the picture is arranged
around two men agreeing that the war is over.

## detail
Almost everyone here has a name — the museum lists two dozen of them, from the ensign
Jacob Banningh sitting beside the big drum down to Willem the drummer — and most of the
objects on the table have survived as well. That is unusual even among civic-guard
portraits, and it is why this canvas is used as evidence about how such dinners actually
looked.

Paper hangs on the drum with a poem by Jan Vos on it, spelling out the occasion for anyone
who needed telling. Through the open windows you can see the front of the brewery 'het
Lam' on the Singel. The painting is dated 1648, but historians think it was finished a
year or more later; van der Helst seems to have dated his group portraits from the
commission, not the completion.
