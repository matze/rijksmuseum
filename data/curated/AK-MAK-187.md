## timeline
Shiva dances inside a ring of fire, balanced on one leg, four arms out, hair flying —
and stands on a dwarf. He is creating the world and destroying it in the same movement.
The bronze was cast in Tamil Nadu, in southern India, in the twelfth century or
thereabouts, and weighs 255 kilos.

## closer
Walk around it if you can. Every angle is resolved, because it was made to be carried
through a crowd.

## detail
This is Shiva as Nataraja, King of Dancers, in the pose called anandatandava. The figure
under his foot is a dwarf, and the dwarf is ignorance. The circle of flames is the
universe he is dancing into and out of existence, which is why the dance is both
creation and destruction and not two separate acts.

Bronzes like this were not made to stand still in a hall. On feast days they were
dressed, garlanded and carried in procession, with poles pushed through the rings cast
into the base. The rings are still there, and they are the reason the whole thing is
balanced the way it is.
