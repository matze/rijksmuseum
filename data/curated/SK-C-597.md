## timeline
She was about twenty, and her family sold arms. Maria Trip stands with one arm on a
balustrade, wearing a collar of the finest lace, a brooch with earrings to match, and a
fortune in pearls — at the ear, at the throat, on the wrists.

## closer
The fan in her left hand is Chinese and folds. In 1639 that was a rare and expensive thing
to be holding.

## detail
Rembrandt gives the jewellery everything he has: each pearl is a stroke of grey and a
stroke of white, and from two metres away they hang and swing. The lace is done the same
way, as pattern implied rather than drawn.

The identification is likely rather than proven, which is why the museum's title says
'probably'. What is certain is the money, and that a painter who could handle it this well
was the one an Amsterdam family of that class would go to.

## look
1. The pearls, first from across the room and then from close up.
2. The folding fan, held out at her hip.
3. The lace collar, and how few actual lines it is made of.
