## timeline
A small portrait, 37 by 23 centimetres, of a soldier in the Royal Netherlands East Indies
Army. He was recruited in Ghana at Elmina, a fortress the Dutch had used for the slave
trade, and given the name Kees Pop. His own name is not known. His parents were called
Kwamena and Ekoerva.

## closer
The two ribbons on his chest. The blue one is the Aceh Medal; the green and orange is the
Cross for Important Military Service.

## detail
He served twelve years and fought in the Aceh War in Sumatra, one of the longest and
bloodiest campaigns of the Dutch colonial era. Isaac Israels painted him in 1882, in the
Netherlands, while he was on sick leave and shortly before he went back to Africa.

Almost everything about him reaches us through Dutch paperwork: a European name given at
a recruiting post, medals awarded by the army he was recruited into, a rank and a
campaign. The portrait is here because the museum now shows that history rather than
storing it.

## look
1. The medals, and what each of them was awarded for.
2. The scale of the panel — this is a picture you have to walk up to.
3. His expression, which the painter did not arrange.
