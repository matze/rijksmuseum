## timeline
A rigged model of a three-masted ship, two metres high, made in 1755 and named on the
stern: De Witte Oliphant, 1755. The museum's own catalogue says what it is likely to be —
a slaver — and says so from the evidence of the model itself.
region: 0.2000 0.5900 0.4900 0.2400

## closer
Look at the two black figures at the corners of the stern. They are carved decoration on
a ship built to carry people.

## detail
Nothing is known about the vessel or about who made the model. The reading rests on three
things the model shows: rigging that is unusually tall for the hull, an unusually wide
beam, and a decorative scheme in which two Africans are carved as the quarter figures at
the stern. Those three are what the museum's catalogue puts its conclusion on.

The rest is a shipwright's document. Gun ports along two decks with every gun missing; a
crowned lion as figurehead; a glass dome over the quarterdeck to light the
cabin below; a six-oared launch hanging from davits at the stern; a steering wheel against
the mizzen mast. The stern also carries the monogram LAL under a crown.
