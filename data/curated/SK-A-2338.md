## timeline
Roses, poppies, irises, carnations and bindweed in a blue glass vase on a marble top,
signed 'Rachel Ruysch 1716'. Ruysch painted flowers professionally for more than six
decades and was famous internationally in her own lifetime — the best documented woman
painter of the Dutch Golden Age.

## closer
Go looking for the insects. The museum's own description says the picture teems with
them, and they are easy to walk past.

## detail
No such bouquet ever stood on a table. There are more than ten species here and they
flower at different times of the year, so the arrangement had to be assembled from study
rather than observed: a year of the garden, compressed into one vase.

The insects are the second subject. Her father, Frederik Ruysch, was a professor of
anatomy and botany with a vast collection of specimens, and she learned to draw on it; a
fly or a beetle gets the same attention here as a petal. Her teacher, Willem van Aelst,
taught her to arrange a bouquet so that it looks unarranged.
