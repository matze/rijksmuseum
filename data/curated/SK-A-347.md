## timeline
A castle on a wooded summit, seen from below at a stream full of rocks and fallen
timber. Bentheim Castle is real and stands just over the German border. The mountain
under it is not: Ruisdael built that himself.

## closer
Two figures on the path give you the scale, and the scale is a lie. Find them, then look
back up at the hill.

## detail
Ruisdael painted this castle about a dozen times, and each time put it on a different
hill. In fact it sits on a low rise. Raising it, wooding the slope and pushing a rocky
torrent across the foreground turns a border town into something closer to the Alps,
and the picture is fantasy and observation woven together with no seam showing. Goethe
called Ruisdael a poet, which is the same observation made politely.

The signature is at the lower right. The canvas is 68 by 54 centimetres — upright, which
is unusual for a landscape and is what lets the hill tower.
