## timeline
A Dutch warship lying in a flat calm, sails hanging slack, firing a single gun. Nothing
is being attacked and nothing is moving. The whole painting is arranged around the cloud
of smoke that one shot makes.

## detail
Van de Velde the Younger grew up in the trade. His father was a marine draughtsman who
made studies of naval actions, and the son worked from them; he was also taught by Simon
de Vlieger and looked hard at Jan van de Cappelle, who painted clouds and their
reflections in still water. All of that is in this picture, spent on stillness: the sails
loosed and empty, the sea a sheet, the hull sitting in its own reflection.

Two sloops lie to either side of the man-of-war, and a second Dutch ship rides in the
distance with its sails down. The picture is the companion to another Van de Velde in
this museum, a ship caught in a squall — the same painter's answer to the opposite
weather.
