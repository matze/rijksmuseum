## timeline
In 1634 Rembrandt travelled to Rotterdam to paint a brewer and his wife: Dirck Pesser and
Haesje van Cleyburg, of the city's Remonstrant elite. She is the one who stayed. Her
husband's portrait is in Los Angeles.

## closer
The ruff. It looks austere until you know that a millstone collar like this took up to
fifteen metres of cloth.

## detail
That is the trick of the picture, and probably of the sitter. Everything reads as
restraint — black cloth, white linen, no jewellery worth mentioning — and everything is
expensive. Sober dress was how a certain kind of Dutch money preferred to be seen.

The two portraits were painted as a pair and hung as a pair, and they have been apart for
long enough that the museum's record has to explain the absence. Buying this one back took
a consortium: the state, the Vereniging Rembrandt, four foundations, a lottery, businesses
and private donors.
