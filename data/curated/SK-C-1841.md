## timeline
Twenty-one centimetres tall. This and the portrait of his wife beside it are the smallest
paintings Rembrandt ever made. Jan Willemsz van der Pluym was sixty-nine, a prosperous
slater and plumber in Leiden who left fifteen houses to his four children.

## closer
Stand close. At this size the ruff is a few strokes and the face is barely more.

## detail
These are family likenesses, not commissions, and the handling is correspondingly free and
sketchy — quite possibly done as a personal favour rather than for a fee. Rembrandt worked
here at the scale of a miniature but with the brush of a full-size portrait.

They come from the Leiden world he grew up in: a tradesman's family with money, painted by
a painter who had already left for Amsterdam. The pair are on loan from the Holterman
family and hang together, which is the only way to see them properly.
