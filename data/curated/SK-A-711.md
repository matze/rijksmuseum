## timeline
Cows standing and lying in a field of pollarded willows at the end of a warm day, with a
farm behind them and a woman milking at the right. Potter painted animals the way other
people painted patrons, and he was dead at twenty-eight.

## closer
Look at the cows as portraits. Each one has a different hide, a different pose, and is
plainly a different animal.

## detail
Potter signed and dated this in 1653 and died the next year. In a short working life he
made cattle the actual subject of pictures rather than the furniture of them, painted at
eye level and given individual character: the black one standing, the red one lying with
its head up, the white one folded down in the shade.

The light is low and coming from the far side of the water, which is what turns the row
of pollarded willows into a screen of separate trunks. A hunter with a dog walks by the
farm. A sheep lies against the fence on the left. The whole population of the meadow is
accounted for.
