## timeline
A wet winter afternoon on a bridge in Amsterdam, and a woman in a veil and a fur-trimmed coat walking straight at you out of the crowd. Breitner painted it in 1896, a metre by a
metre and a half — the scale of a history painting, spent on people crossing a road.

## closer
Notice where the edges cut. Figures are sliced off at the frame as if nobody had waited to
be composed.

## detail
Breitner worked from his own photographs, and the picture shows it: the woman coming
head-on, the arbitrary cropping, the moment caught rather than arranged. That is what a
camera taught painting to do, and he was among the first Dutch painters to take the lesson.

She was not always a lady. Breitner first painted her as a maid, and changed her into an
elegant woman after a bad review and a request from his dealer. The costume is a
correction; the walk is the original.
