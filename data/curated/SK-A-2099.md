## timeline
A bundle of white asparagus tied with string, on a stone ledge, against black. Twenty-five
centimetres tall and painted on paper. In a century that liked its still lifes loaded
with silver and lobsters, Coorte painted one vegetable and stopped.

## closer

## detail
Very little is known about Coorte. He is thought to have been born near Middelburg, spent
about 1680 in Amsterdam as a pupil of Melchior d'Hondecoeter — whose enormous bird pieces
hang two rooms away — and was back in Middelburg by 1683, signing small still lifes for
the next twenty-four years. What survives is a run of them: asparagus, strawberries,
gooseberries, apricots, shells, each one object on a plinth in a dark with no room in it
at all. Six hang in this museum.

The fashion at the time ran the other way, towards abundance: silver, porcelain, fruit
piled to the edge of the panel. These are the argument against that, and they were painted
at the same moment. He signed and dated this one on the front of the plinth, in 1697.
