## timeline
On the night of 18–19 November 1421 the sea came through the dikes. Around Dordrecht 23
villages went under and some 2,000 people died. This panel shows the breach at the right
and the town, undamaged, at the left, with the drowned villages named across the water.

## closer
It is a map as much as a painting. Read the flooded landscape village by village and see
how much of it is water.

## detail
The survivors commissioned an altarpiece for the Grote Kerk in Dordrecht to remember what
had happened, and this is its outer left wing; scenes from the life of Saint Elizabeth of
Hungary are painted on the other side. Disaster reporting and devotional painting were the
same object.

Nobody knows who painted it. The artist is called the Master of the St Elizabeth Panels
after this commission, and is dated by it to about 1490 to 1495 — some seventy years after
the flood itself, which is to say within living memory of nobody.

## look
1. The break in the dike at the right, where the water is coming through.
2. Dordrecht at the left, dry, with the Vuilpoort gate.
3. The rescues in the foreground: goods being loaded from a boat onto a cart.

## kids
Count the church towers standing in the water. Each one was a village.
