## timeline
Mondrian before the grid, and openly in debt to the Hague School. A few years earlier he
had sat in this museum copying Paul Gabriël's *In the Month of July*, which hangs in this
same gallery. The compositions are close relatives; the effect is nothing alike.

## closer
Compare the two paintings in the room. Same country, same kind of mill — one of them is
describing a place, the other is arranging one.

## detail
What Mondrian changed were the lines and the colours, deliberately, to get away from
description. The result is flatter, more decorative and less real than its model, and that
was the intention.

Seen from the far end of his career the picture reads as a first move: the landscape is
still recognisable, but it is already being treated as a set of shapes that can be tuned.

## look
1. The mill's silhouette, simplified almost to a flat shape.
2. The reflection in the Gein, handled as pattern rather than water.
3. Gabriël's painting elsewhere in this gallery, for the comparison Mondrian invited.
